KioskBrowser (H2 Kiosk Browser)

KioskBrowser ("the app") is developed by H2-AI GmbH, Germany. It locks a tablet to one website, for example in a shop, at a reception desk or at a trade fair stand. This privacy policy explains what the app does with data, what stays on the device and what leaves it.

Controller: H2-AI GmbH, Amselrain 43, 75038 Oberderdingen, Germany, Email: info@h2-ai.de

The short version: the tablet loads the website that the person who set it up has chosen, and everything that is shown or entered there goes directly between the tablet and that website. We do not receive, store or analyse any of it. The only thing that reaches us is what we need for the license: a pseudonymous device ID and, after a purchase, the Google Play purchase receipt. If you only use the free demo, the app sends the pseudonymous device ID to us once, for a check after installation, and nothing after that. There is no account, no analytics and no advertising.

You can enter a creator code before buying. To check it, the app sends only the code to our server, without any device ID (section 3 d), "Creator codes"). Business customers can also obtain licenses directly from us instead of through Google Play (section 3 d), "Licenses bought directly from us").

If you pair a phone with our free app H2 Kiosk Control, the tablet exchanges its status and commands with that phone, directly in your local Wi-Fi (section 3 e). If you also turn on remote access, this exchange can run through our relay server, which passes it on end-to-end encrypted and cannot read it (section 3 f).

1. What we collect

We collect only the license data described in section 3 d): a pseudonymous device ID, the purchase receipt after you buy a license, a creator code if you enter one, and the technical server logs of our license server. If you write to us, for example with "Email support", we also receive your email (section 3 d), "Support requests by email"). Business customers who buy licenses directly from us also give us their order details (section 3 d), "Licenses bought directly from us"). The app contains no analytics, no crash reporting and no advertising libraries of any kind. Nothing from the websites shown on the tablet, nothing that visitors enter, and nothing from your settings is ever sent to us. If you turn on remote access for central management, our relay server also processes the tablet's IP address and the identifiers needed to connect it, only in working memory, essentially only while the connection lasts (section 3 f).

2. What stays on the device

The following is stored locally on the device and is never transmitted to us:

  • Your settings, including the start page, the list of additional allowed addresses, the lock, session, screensaver, schedule and orientation options, stored with Android DataStore
  • Your exit PIN, stored as a salted hash (PBKDF2), never in readable form
  • What the built-in browser keeps for the pages it shows, such as cookies, page storage, cache, form entries and history, as any browser does. With the visitor session reset switched on (the default), this is deleted, and the clipboard is emptied, after the idle time you set and when the restart button is used with "Also reset the session" (section 4)
  • If you use the picture slideshow, the permission to read the one folder you picked. The pictures are read from that folder and shown on the screen, nothing else
  • If you bought a license: the license for this device as a file signed by our server, so the full version keeps working offline, and until the license is confirmed, the Google Play purchase receipt
  • If you bought with a creator code: the code, until the purchase has been registered with our server. The code is sent to our server as described in section 3 d)
  • Technical messages in Android's system log on the device. In the published app, web addresses appear there only as the website's origin (for example https://example.com/…), without path or parameters. These messages are not sent to us
  • If you use central management with H2 Kiosk Control: the pairing data described in section 3 e)

The app is excluded from Android's cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer, so none of this data is copied into a Google backup.

d) Our license server

The full version is sold as a license for one device. To bind the license to the tablet and to check it, the app talks to our license server at lizenz.h2-ai.de, always over HTTPS.

When the app contacts it:

  • Once after the app is installed: when the app starts and no license is stored on the device, it asks our server whether this device has a license, so that a license bought for this device comes back without you having to do anything, for example after reinstalling the app. This check waits until the device is online. Once our server has answered (license found or no license), the app does not repeat this automatic check. If there was no answer, the app tries again the next time it starts.
  • Right after you buy a license, to register the purchase. The same happens when the app finds a purchase of this license in Google Play that was made outside the app, for example with a promo code redeemed in the Play Store, and this device can claim it (see below).
  • About once a day while a license is stored on the device and the device is online, to renew it. Without a connection, the full version keeps working for up to 30 days from the last successful check; after 20 days the app reminds you to connect.
  • When you tap "Restore license", for example if the automatic check could not reach our server.
  • When you enter a creator code and tap "Apply", to check the code. This request contains only the code and the name of the app, no device ID (see "Creator codes" below).

The app does not contact the license server in any other situation. If you never buy a license, the app sends the pseudonymous device ID to us only for the one automatic check after installation. Showing the device ID in the settings ("Show device ID") works on the device only and sends nothing.

To find purchases made outside the app, the app asks Google Play on the device (not our server) for the purchases of the device's Google account when it starts and when it comes back to the foreground, at most once a minute. This query goes to Google Play only. Only a purchase that this device can claim is then registered with our server.

What is sent:

  • Device ID: a SHA-256 hash of Android's device ID (ANDROID_ID) combined with a fixed value specific to this app. Our server never receives the Android ID itself. The value stays the same when you reinstall the app and changes after a factory reset.
  • Purchase receipt, only when a purchase is registered: the purchase token that Google Play gives the app, and the ID of the product bought.
  • The name of the app ("kioskbrowser").
  • Creator code, only if you entered one: when the code is checked, and again together with the purchase receipt when the purchase is registered.

When our server receives a purchase token, it asks Google Play to confirm the purchase and receives from Google the order number, the quantity, the purchase state, whether it was a test purchase, the device ID that the app passed to Google Play when you bought (only for a purchase made in the app; section 8), the time of the purchase and, if you bought at a discount, which discount offer was used. It stores the device ID, the purchase token, the product ID, the order number, the quantity, whether it was a test purchase, whether the purchase has been consumed, and the time of registration. For each license it keeps a license ID, the device it is bound to and when it was bound, withdrawn or moved, and a log of these events. It then marks the purchase as consumed at Google Play. Our server also regularly fetches from Google Play the list of refunded purchases of this app (purchase token, order number, time, quantity, reason) and keeps it, so that a refunded purchase cannot become a license.

The license the server sends back contains the device ID, the license ID and how long it is valid, and is signed by our server. It is stored on the device (section 2).

Why, and on which legal basis:

  • To confirm that a purchase is genuine and bind it to the device it was bought on, and to renew and restore the license (performance of the contract, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • To stop misuse: a purchase can only be registered once and for one device only (the device it was bought on, or, for a purchase made outside the app without a device ID, the first device that registers it), and a license is withdrawn when Google Play reports that the purchase was refunded (legitimate interest in preventing fraud, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
  • To move a license to the same device after a factory reset, if you ask us to (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • To give a license back to the device it was bought for without you having to ask, through the automatic check after installation. If the device has a license, this serves the contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). If it has none, the check is based on our legitimate interest in returning licenses to our customers automatically and in not offering a second purchase for a device that is already licensed (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The answer "no license" is not stored on our server.
  • To check a creator code and to count a purchase made with it for the creator who handed out the code, so that we can pay the creator for the purchases made with their code (legitimate interest in working with creators and settling their commission, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; see "Creator codes" below).
  • Reading the Android device ID on the device is only done in the situations listed above, and when you tap "Show device ID" in the settings or "Email support". In our view recognising the device, including restoring a license bought for it after a reinstallation, is strictly necessary to provide the licensed service (Section 25(2) no. 2 of the German TDDDG). The device ID is used for nothing else.

Server logs: our license server receives the device's IP address with each request. The web server writes it to its access log together with the time, the requested address, the response code and the technical identification that Android sends with each request (Android version, device model and build number). The access log is deleted after at most 15 days and does not contain the device ID. We use it only to operate the server and to fend off attacks; we do not use it to determine your location and do not combine it with the license data. To limit abuse, the server also counts requests per IP address for one minute, in memory only. The license service itself writes no access log and never logs device IDs, purchase tokens or order numbers.

How long:

  • Records of a license that can still be used are kept as long as the license exists.
  • Records of refunded or withdrawn licenses are deleted 12 months after the last withdrawal, so that the same purchase cannot be registered again.
  • Entries in the event log, and refunds reported by Google for purchases that were never registered, are deleted after 12 months.
  • A check that finds no license is not stored in our database; only the access log entry remains.
  • The link between a purchase and the creator code it was made with is kept as long as the purchase record and deleted together with it.
  • Checking a creator code is not stored in our database; only the access log entry remains.
  • Licenses we grant directly to business customers (see "Licenses bought directly from us" below) are kept and deleted like licenses bought through Google Play.
  • Encrypted backups of the license database are kept for 30 days, server access logs for at most 15 days.

Deletion runs automatically once a day.

e) Central management with H2 Kiosk Control

Central management is optional and off until you pair a phone. With our free app H2 Kiosk Control you can manage this tablet, and your other H2 kiosk devices, from your phone. In the same Wi-Fi, the tablet and the phone connect directly, with no server of ours in between. If you turn on remote access, the connection can also run through our relay server (section 3 f). In both cases the status and the commands are end-to-end encrypted between the tablet and your phone: neither H2-AI GmbH nor anyone else can read them. There is no account.

Pairing: a phone can only be paired while kiosk mode is off, so while the kiosk is running only after your exit PIN has been entered. Tap "Control all your devices from your phone" on the start screen (or Settings > Central management) and then "Pair a phone". The tablet shows a QR code and an 8-digit code, valid for 5 minutes and for one pairing. After 5 wrong codes the code is blocked and only the QR code still works, until you start pairing again. The QR code contains the tablet's address and port in the local network, the random installation ID (see below), the type of app, the fingerprint of its certificate, a one-time secret, the tablet's name, the expiry time and, if remote access is on, the address of our relay server. If you type the 8-digit code instead, the tablet and the phone both show a 6-digit number to compare, and you confirm it on the tablet with "Number matches".

What the tablet stores, in the app's own storage, which is excluded from backup:

  • A random ID of this app installation. It is not the device ID used for the license (section 3 d) and is not derived from the Android ID
  • The tablet's name (at first the model name Android reports for the tablet) and the settings "Allow remote unlock" and "Remote access"
  • The tablet's own certificate for these connections; the password that protects it is sealed with the Android Keystore
  • For each paired phone: an ID chosen by the phone, the name the phone sends from H2 Kiosk Control (the phone's manufacturer and model as Android reports them), the fingerprint of its certificate, when it was paired and last seen, and an access ticket for remote access (a random value that this phone also receives)

What the tablet sends to paired phones: its random installation ID and name, the app version, whether kiosk mode is on and ready to start, whether a license is active or the demo is running, whether the screen is on, the start page, the address currently shown (only while kiosk mode is running) and whether it loaded, the navigation lock and the additional allowed addresses, the screen schedule, the battery level and whether it is charging, whether "Allow remote unlock" and "Remote access" are on and the state of the connection to our relay server, the tablet's time and time zone, and how long the app has been running.

What paired phones can do: change the tablet's name, start page, navigation lock, allowed addresses and screen schedule, switch kiosk mode on and, if "Allow remote unlock" is on (the default), off, switch the screen off and on, reload the page, switch remote access on or off, and remove themselves from the tablet. They can never change the exit PIN, the accessibility service, permissions or the license, reset the device or control other apps. Remote commands never make the accessibility service act. The tablet keeps the last 200 remote commands (time, name of the phone, command, result, whether it came through our relay server, no page content) only in working memory, where they are lost when the app is closed, and notes them as technical messages in Android's system log on the tablet; neither is sent to us.

Security: the connection is encrypted (TLS), and the tablet and the phone each check the other's certificate. Only phones you have paired can read the status or send commands. Any other device in the same network can at most open an encrypted connection, try to pair (which needs the code shown on the tablet) and learn the tablet's name, its random installation ID and the app and its version. Through our relay server, a connection without a valid access ticket is closed at once, and pairing is only possible while a pairing window is open on the tablet. So that the app can find the tablet, the tablet announces itself in the local network while central management is on, as network printers do. This announcement contains only a neutral service name ("H2 Kiosk" and the first characters of the random installation ID), the random installation ID, the type of app, the protocol version, whether pairing is open and a short part of its certificate fingerprint. It is not encrypted, and other devices in the same network can see it. The tablet's name is not part of it. If the tablet or the phone uses Android 9, the certificates exchanged when a connection is set up in the Wi-Fi are not encrypted either; they contain the random installation ID. The status and the commands are always encrypted. While a phone is paired or a pairing window is open, a background service keeps the tablet reachable (section 6).

Ending it: tap "Remove" next to a phone in Central management; that phone can then no longer control the tablet, and its access ticket is no longer valid. A phone can also remove itself from the tablet in H2 Kiosk Control. When the last phone is removed, the tablet stops the service, closes the connection point, ends its connection to our relay server and no longer announces itself. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android's app settings, deletes all of the data above from the tablet. What H2 Kiosk Control keeps on your phone is described in that app's privacy policy.

Apart from what our relay server processes for remote access (section 3 f), these data stay on your own devices and never reach us; you control them on your tablet and your phone.

f) Remote access through our relay server (optional)

Remote access lets your paired phones reach this tablet when they are not in the same Wi-Fi, for example on mobile data. It is off by default. You switch it on under "Remote access" in Central management, which can only be changed while kiosk mode is off; after the first pairing the app asks you once whether to turn it on. A paired phone can also switch it on or off from H2 Kiosk Control. Remote access is available on tablets with Android 10 or newer; on Android 9 the tablet does not connect to our relay server.

How it works: while remote access is on and a phone is paired or a pairing window is open, the tablet keeps one connection to our relay server and sends a short signal about every 4 minutes to keep it open. When a paired phone wants to connect, the relay tells the tablet, the tablet opens a second connection, and the relay joins it with the phone's connection. Inside, the tablet and the phone use exactly the same encrypted connection as in the Wi-Fi (section 3 e): both check each other's certificate, and only the tablet and the phone hold the keys. The relay only passes the encrypted data on. It cannot read the status, the commands or the pages shown, and it cannot send commands.

What the relay processes:

  • The tablet's IP address (the public address of your internet connection), as with any internet connection
  • The tablet's certificate for these connections, with the random installation ID it contains (section 3 e; not the device ID used for the license), and the type of app. The certificate identifies the tablet to the relay, so nobody else can register in its place
  • One hash for each paired phone's access ticket (at most 16), so that the relay lets only your paired phones through. This also shows how many phones are paired. While a pairing window is open: hashes derived from the QR code and from the 8-digit code, not the codes themselves
  • When the tablet is online, when phones connect, and how much encrypted data is passed on

If a phone with Android 9 connects, the encryption standard available there means that the relay could technically see the certificates of the phone and the tablet that are exchanged inside the connection; it does not evaluate or store them. Nothing is sent unencrypted on the way between the devices and the relay.

How long: the relay keeps these data only in working memory and only while the tablet's connection lasts. When it ends, the relay forgets the tablet, apart from the tablet's certificate fingerprint and the time, which it keeps for up to one minute to limit repeated reconnects. To protect the service from abuse, it counts connections per IP address in working memory; this count is deleted no more than about 3 minutes after the last connection. Attempts to pair with the 8-digit code through the relay are also counted, in working memory only; this count is deleted after about one hour. The relay stores nothing on disk and keeps no logs of IP addresses, installation IDs or tickets. Its technical log contains only totals (for example the number of open connections) and start errors; only if the service crashes can the crash report contain fragments of such data. The log is kept in working memory only, is limited in size, so that older entries are removed, and is deleted completely when the server restarts.

Where: the relay runs at relay.h2-ai.de, on a server operated by H2-AI GmbH and hosted for us by Hetzner Online GmbH in a data centre in Germany, which processes the data on our behalf under a data processing agreement. The relay is not connected to our license server, and the data is not combined with license data.

Why, and on which legal basis: to provide remote access, which you switched on (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR), and to protect the relay against abuse and attacks through the per-address limits (legitimate interest in a secure service, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Turning it off: switch off "Remote access" on the tablet or from a paired phone; switching off always works. The tablet then closes its connection to the relay at once, and the relay forgets the tablet. Removing a phone makes its access ticket invalid immediately; when the last phone is removed and no pairing window is open, the connection ends. If no paired phone uses the tablet for 30 days, the tablet ends the connection by itself ("sleeping") until a paired phone connects in the Wi-Fi, a pairing window is opened or remote access is switched on again.

4. Visitor sessions, uploads and downloads

The tablet is often used by many people one after another. With "Reset session when idle" switched on (the default), the app deletes cookies, sign-ins, page storage, form entries, cache, saved HTTP logins, certificate and security choices, location permissions given to websites, the clipboard and the browsing history after the idle time you set, and loads the start page again. The same happens when the restart button is used with "Also reset the session". If you switch the reset off ("keep the session"), for example so that staff stay signed in, the next visitor can see what the previous one left in the website.

File uploads are switched off by default. If you switch them on, a website can ask for a file; the visitor picks it in Android's system file or photo picker, and the file goes directly to that website. The system picker may also show storage that is connected to the device, such as a cloud drive of the device's Google account. Check what the picker shows before you allow uploads on a public tablet.

The app has no camera, microphone or location permission. Downloads are always blocked, so no file from a website is saved on the tablet.

5. Permissions

  • Accessibility service: used only to keep the kiosk locked while kiosk mode is running. It detects which app or system window comes to the front and puts the kiosk back, blocks the Home and Recents keys, counts Volume Up presses for the exit gesture, and closes the notification shade and the power menu, each only if that option is switched on. To tell the notification shade from the power menu, it looks at the titles and button labels of these system windows at the moment they open. Of other apps it only uses which app is in front. It lets the system call screen (answering and ending calls) and the confirmation window of Google Play services for reading a verification SMS appear in front of the kiosk; the app only recognises these windows by their name, has no phone or SMS permission and does not read, store or send any call or message content. It does not read the content of the website or of other apps, and it stores and transmits nothing.
  • Display over other apps: needed for the screensaver, the PIN dialog and, on Android 11 and older, the status bar block.
  • Usage access: used to check which app is in the foreground, as a second, independent path for the kiosk lock.
  • Start at boot: so the kiosk comes back by itself after a power cut.
  • Keep awake: so the tablet does not fall asleep.
  • Modify system settings: only requested if you choose a fixed orientation, and used only to set the system rotation lock.
  • Internet and network state: to load the website, check the license and, if you pair a phone, to be reachable for H2 Kiosk Control in the local Wi-Fi and, with remote access on, through our relay server (sections 3 e and 3 f).

These permissions are used solely to run the kiosk. None of them is used to collect data.

6. Background services

While kiosk mode is running, one background service checks which app is in the foreground and brings the kiosk back. Scheduled alarms run the nightly reload and the screen schedule, and the license check runs as described in section 3 d). Apart from the license requests, nothing is sent to us.

If you pair a phone with H2 Kiosk Control, a second background service keeps the tablet reachable for paired phones, also while kiosk mode is off and after a restart: in the local Wi-Fi and, with remote access on, through its connection to our relay server (section 3 f). It runs only while a phone is paired or a pairing window is open, and stops when the last phone is removed (section 3 e). Android may show the notification "Central management on" while it runs.

7. Transport security

The connection to our license server is always encrypted (HTTPS); the app refuses any other connection to it. Whether the connection to the website you show is encrypted depends on that website: the app also accepts http:// and local network addresses, for example for an intranet page. We recommend https:// wherever possible.

Central management (sections 3 e and 3 f): the status and the commands are always end-to-end encrypted between the tablet and your phone. The connection to our relay server is encrypted as well, and the app only accepts the relay if its key matches one built into the app. All connections to servers are encrypted. Only within your local network, and not to any server, two things are sent unencrypted: the tablet's announcement and, if the tablet or the phone uses Android 9, the certificates exchanged when a connection is set up in the Wi-Fi (section 3 e).

10. Data deletion

To request deletion or a copy of the license data we hold, email info@h2-ai.de. Please give the Google Play order number (it starts with "GPA." and is in the receipt email from Google Play), the device ID, or both; for a license bought directly from us, our invoice or order reference. The app shows the device ID in its settings under "Device ID (for license support)": tap "Show device ID", then "Copy". We answer within one month.

  • With the order number (or our invoice or order reference), we delete the purchase with all its licenses, refunds, log entries and its link to a creator code. The license ends and cannot be restored.
  • With the device ID only, we end the license on that device and remove the device ID from all our records. The purchase itself stays recorded without the device ID, so that the same purchase cannot be registered again.

Copies in encrypted backups disappear within 30 days. Google Play keeps its own records of the purchase. Our relay server keeps no data beyond the connection, apart from short-lived abuse counters in working memory (section 3 f), so there is nothing to delete there.

To remove everything the app has stored on the device, uninstall it, or clear its storage in Android's app settings. Data that a website stored on its own servers can only be deleted by that website's operator.

f) Fernzugriff über unseren Vermittlungsserver (freiwillig)

Mit dem Fernzugriff erreichen Ihre gekoppelten Handys dieses Tablet auch dann, wenn sie nicht im selben WLAN sind, zum Beispiel über mobile Daten. Ab Werk ist er aus. Der Fernzugriff steht auf Tablets ab Android 10 zur Verfügung; unter Android 9 verbindet sich das Tablet nicht mit unserem Vermittlungsserver.

So funktioniert es: Solange der Fernzugriff an ist und ein Handy gekoppelt oder ein Kopplungsfenster offen ist, hält das Tablet eine Verbindung zu unserem Vermittlungsserver und sendet etwa alle 4 Minuten ein kurzes Signal, damit sie offen bleibt. Will sich ein gekoppeltes Handy verbinden, meldet der Vermittler das dem Tablet, das Tablet öffnet eine zweite Verbindung, und der Vermittler verbindet sie mit der Verbindung des Handys. Darin nutzen Tablet und Handy genau dieselbe verschlüsselte Verbindung wie im WLAN (Abschnitt 3 e): Beide prüfen das Zertifikat des anderen, und nur Tablet und Handy haben die Schlüssel. Der Vermittler reicht die verschlüsselten Daten nur weiter. Er kann Status, Befehle und angezeigte Seiten nicht lesen und keine Befehle senden.

Was der Vermittler verarbeitet:

  • Die IP-Adresse des Tablets (die öffentliche Adresse Ihres Internetanschlusses), wie bei jeder Internetverbindung
  • Das Zertifikat des Tablets für diese Verbindungen mit der darin enthaltenen zufälligen Installationskennung (Abschnitt 3 e; nicht die Gerätekennung für die Lizenz) und die Art der App. Mit dem Zertifikat weist sich das Tablet beim Vermittler aus, damit sich niemand an seiner Stelle anmelden kann
  • Je gekoppeltem Handy einen Hash seines Zugangsscheins (höchstens 16), damit der Vermittler nur Ihre gekoppelten Handys durchlässt. Daraus geht auch hervor, wie viele Handys gekoppelt sind. Während ein Kopplungsfenster offen ist: aus dem QR-Code und aus dem 8-stelligen Code abgeleitete Hashes, nicht die Codes selbst
  • Wann das Tablet online ist, wann sich Handys verbinden und wie viele verschlüsselte Daten weitergereicht werden

Verbindet sich ein Handy mit Android 9, könnte der Vermittler wegen des dort verfügbaren Verschlüsselungsstandards technisch die Zertifikate von Handy und Tablet sehen, die innerhalb der Verbindung ausgetauscht werden; er wertet sie nicht aus und speichert sie nicht. Auf dem Weg zwischen den Geräten und dem Vermittler wird nichts unverschlüsselt übertragen.

Wie lange: Der Vermittler hält diese Daten nur im Arbeitsspeicher und nur, solange die Verbindung des Tablets besteht. Endet sie, vergisst er das Tablet, bis auf den Fingerabdruck des Tablet-Zertifikats und die Uhrzeit, die er bis zu einer Minute behält, um wiederholte Neuanmeldungen zu begrenzen. Zum Schutz vor Missbrauch zählt er Verbindungen je IP-Adresse im Arbeitsspeicher; dieser Zähler wird spätestens etwa 3 Minuten nach der letzten Verbindung gelöscht. Auch Kopplungsversuche mit dem 8-stelligen Code über den Vermittler zählt er, nur im Arbeitsspeicher; dieser Zähler wird nach etwa einer Stunde gelöscht. Der Vermittler speichert nichts auf einem Datenträger und führt keine Protokolle mit IP-Adressen, Installationskennungen oder Zugangsscheinen. Sein technisches Protokoll enthält nur Summen (zum Beispiel die Zahl offener Verbindungen) und Startfehler; nur wenn der Dienst abstürzt, kann der Absturzbericht Bruchstücke solcher Daten enthalten. Das Protokoll liegt nur im Arbeitsspeicher, ist in der Größe begrenzt, sodass ältere Einträge entfallen, und wird bei einem Neustart des Servers vollständig gelöscht.
